**Radiohead: Digital album sales, 1993–2011**

This chart shows the **results** of a **study** **carried out** by Nielsen SoundScan into digital sales of Radiohead’s albums. The study compares nine releases from 1993 to 2011, and it counts both pure album sales and track downloads converted into album equivalents.

Overall, the study **shows** that the band’s digital sales reached their peak with _OK Computer_ (1997), which sold 2.56 million copies, or 3.4 million when track downloads are included. This is more than **double** the figure for _Pablo Honey_ (1.52 million) and _The Bends_ (1.54 million). Before that, the sales of the first two albums were almost the same, with only a **slight** difference between them.

After the peak, there was a **sharp drop**. _Kid A_ sold 1.48 million copies, and _Amnesiac_ fell to 1.02 million. The lowest point came with the live album _I Might Be Wrong_, which sold only 298,000. Then the numbers recovered: _Hail to the Thief_ reached 1.12 million, and _In Rainbows_ stayed **fairly constant** at 1.02 million. However, _The King of Limbs_ (2011) fell again, to just 370,000.

In summary, Radiohead’s digital sales have shown a **significant decline** since 1997. Interestingly, the **greatest fall** was between _OK Computer_ and _I Might Be Wrong_, when sales went down by more than 2 million copies.
